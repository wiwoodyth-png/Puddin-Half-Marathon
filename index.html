Coming back from runner's knee — how this plan is built

You went from ~zero running to ramping fast in June, which is what triggered the patellofemoral pain (runner's knee). Since mid-July you've rebuilt cleanly back up to a 5.3 mi long run — this plan starts from there, not from zero.

  • No long run jumps more than ~1–1.5 mi at a time, with real cutback/taper weeks (Aug 30 and Sep 27) so the knee actually adapts instead of just accumulating strain.
  • Monday strength work targets the hip/glute weakness that's the usual root cause of runner’s knee — tap any Monday "Strength" tile for the exact routine (Sydnie’s ankle/calf work is folded into the same tile).
  • Correction: an earlier version of this section guessed you might be hypermobile based on one flexibility test — turned out to be wrong (you can't hyperextend fingers, thumbs, elbows, or knees, and never had it as a kid). The simpler explanation stands: years without strength training plus a fast June ramp is the classic cause, no exotic tissue story needed. The strength work matters just as much either way.
  • Two days off from running every week (Fri/Sat) — Friday now carries a light core+mobility session, Saturday stays fully off — and your last hard running effort before any long run is always at least 2 days out.
  • If knee pain returns: don't push through it. Drop that week's long run by ~20%, skip the speed session, and stick to easy running only until it's pain-free for 2-3 days.
  • Paces above are estimated from your recent training (~10 min/mile easy). Tap any workout for the specific target pace — a 1-mile time trial in week 1 would sharpen these further, but effort-based cues (comfortably hard, 5K effort, etc.) work fine without one.

Sydnie's calf tightness — what's likely going on

Tightness building through the whole calf on long runs specifically, sharp pain, fully resolves within a couple days — that pattern points at calf/Achilles overload rather than anything acute. Most likely cause: limited ankle dorsiflexion (how far the shin can travel over the foot) forcing the calf to do extra work on every push-off, which adds up over a long distance. The ankle-stretching recommendation Sydnie already got is exactly the right instinct — tap any Monday "Strength" tile or Friday "Core" tile for the full routine (ankle stretches + eccentric heel drops, which is the best-evidenced fix for this specific pattern).

Two adjustments worth making: Sydnie's long runs should be run at her easy pace, not matched to Woody's — the long run is specifically what's triggering this, so effort matters more than keeping pace together. And a sharp pain mid-run is a stop-and-walk signal, not a push-through signal.

Worth an actual PT visit if: it stops fully resolving in a couple days, there's any swelling or bruising, or it starts happening even walking or at rest. Nothing described so far sounds like that, but good to know the line.

Extras — cross-training, fueling, race week

Peloton as a bonus, not a replacement: on top of the plan above, an easy 20-30 min zone-2 spin (conversational effort, low resistance) is a good low-impact way to add cardio without adding pounding on the joints — fits well Wednesday or Saturday if either of you wants extra movement without extra running.

Hydration/fueling: once long runs pass 60-75 min (Aug 18 onward), bring water and start practicing race-day fueling — a gel or a few chews around the halfway point. Test whatever you plan to use on race day during a long run first; never try something new on race morning.

Shoes: if your current running shoes have more than ~300-400 miles on them, replace them before race day — worn-out cushioning is a quiet contributor to both knee and calf overuse. Break in any new pair on easy runs only, never for the first time on a long run or race day.

Race week (week of Sep 28): mileage drops on purpose — don't add extra runs to compensate for a "light" feeling week, that's the taper working as intended. Lay out race-day gear (bib, watch charged, shoes, weather-appropriate layers) the night before. Familiarize yourselves with Wilder Ranch parking/start-line logistics a few days ahead so race morning isn't the first time you're figuring that out.

Race morning: eat a familiar breakfast 2-3 hr before the gun, arrive with enough buffer for parking and a short warm-up walk/jog, and start conservatively — the first mile of a race always feels easier than it is.
